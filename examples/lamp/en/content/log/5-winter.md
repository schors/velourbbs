# A long winter evening

The longest evening of the year. It got dark before four,
and I lit the lamp early. The street was empty and white.

The first caller came at five. We read the rules together,
because it was their first visit, and then they asked
how the station began. I told them about the old desk,
the second-hand modem and the lamp from my grandmother's house.
The lamp was the first thing on the desk. The computer came later.

At seven, three callers were on the line at once. That is a record
for a small station. They did not talk much. Each of them read
a text and left a short word in the log. One wrote "warm here".
Another wrote only a star.

Later the snow stopped, and the moon came out over the roofs.
I made tea and read the old notes in this log, from the first night on.
The handwriting changes, the lamp does not.

At midnight the line was quiet. I turned the wick down,
but I did not put the lamp out. Somebody can call at two in the morning,
when the house is asleep and the snow is blue. The station must look awake
for them. A light in the window is a kind of answer
before the question. So the lamp stays on till morning,
as the main screen says, and tonight it is true.
